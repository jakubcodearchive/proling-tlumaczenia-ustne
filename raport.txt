If it’s in a different folder (e.g., pages/tlumaczenia.php):

<a href="pages/tlumaczenia.php">Tłumaczenia symultaniczne</a>

✅ Tip: Always use relative paths for internal links (so they work regardless of domain).
When you upload your website, / will represent your web root (e.g. https://twojadomena.pl/).

✅ 1. Repeating keywords — is it OK?

Yes, repetition is fine, as long as it’s natural.
Google’s current algorithms (especially after the Helpful Content and Core updates) don’t reward mechanical keyword stuffing. Instead, they focus on semantic relevance and topical depth — meaning that it’s better to use natural variations and related terms than to repeat the exact phrase every time.

For example, instead of repeating just:

“tłumaczenia symultaniczne Wrocław”

you can use variations like:

„tłumaczenia konferencyjne Wrocław”

„biuro tłumaczeń we Wrocławiu”

„tłumaczenie kabinowe Wrocław”

„tłumaczenia ustne w woj. dolnośląskim”

💡 Tip:
Use the main keyword once in the <h1> and once in the first 100–150 words, then sprinkle synonyms naturally across the page.
That gives you healthy keyword density (around 1.5–2.5%) without sounding robotic.

✅ 2. Are our current HTML structures good for SEO?

Yes — your current structure is actually very strong:

You’re using semantic headings (<h1>, <h2>, <h3>) logically and hierarchically.

Important phrases are wrapped in <strong> tags — that’s perfect, because it tells Google: “this part of the text is significant.”

You’re using <p> tags for paragraphs, <ul> for lists — that improves structure and readability for both users and search engines.

Just be careful not to overuse <strong> — once per paragraph or section is ideal.
